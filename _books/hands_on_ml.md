One of the best practical machine learning books out there. Geron strikes a rare balance between depth and accessibility — you get real intuition for how algorithms work without drowning in theory, and every concept is grounded in working code you can actually run and experiment with.

The first half, covering Scikit-Learn and classical ML, is excellent: clean explanations of the full modeling pipeline from preprocessing and feature engineering through cross-validation and hyperparameter tuning. The second half steps into deep learning, building up from the fundamentals of neural networks to CNNs, RNNs, and beyond. The progression feels natural rather than overwhelming.

What sets this book apart is how it treats best practices as first-class citizens — not afterthoughts. Topics like regularization, early stopping, and model evaluation are woven throughout rather than tacked on at the end. After finishing this, machine learning stopped feeling like magic and started feeling like engineering.
