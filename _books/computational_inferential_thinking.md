A well-structured introduction to data science that balances Python programming with statistical reasoning. The book's strength is its hands-on approach — nearly every concept is paired with real datasets and runnable code, which makes abstract ideas like hypothesis testing and bootstrapping feel tangible rather than purely theoretical. The chapters on inference were especially illuminating, building intuition for randomness and sampling distributions before introducing formal frameworks.

Some sections feel dense on first read, particularly around permutation testing and confidence intervals, but the payoff is worth it. The progression from tables and visualizations all the way through prediction and classification is logical and well-paced. It avoids hand-waving without becoming inaccessible — a hard balance to strike in an introductory text.

A solid foundation for anyone stepping into data science for the first time.
